# Hero loop — agents.alphaceo.ai
**4s seamless · 16:9 · muted · no text, no logos · <300KB**

## The one idea
A woman buried in paper is feeding it, one sheet at a time, into the open cases of four specialists who are already standing there waiting.

## The first three seconds, muted
A hard tungsten pool in the middle of a dark warehouse. One small seated figure. A paper skyline above her head. Four green seams of light receding into the black on the right. A white sheet lifts off the stack and travels right. That's second one. It happens again at second 1.3 and again at 2.7.

---

## 1. The single frame to generate first

**Camera.** Locked. 40mm full-frame equivalent, f/2.8, lens height 2.6m, tilted 18° down, subject distance 5.0m. No move, at all — see §4. Generate 1920×1080, master and deliver 1600×900.

**Layout, as percentages from the left edge:**

| % across | What is there |
|---|---|
| 0–33 | **Headline dead zone.** Bare concrete floor receding. One rack upright as a soft dark vertical at 31%. Nothing legible, nothing moving. Everything here below 8% luminance. |
| 36–66 | Battered steel desk, rotated ~25° so its near-right corner is closest to camera. |
| 47 | Her head. 38% down from the top. Head occupies ~7% of frame height. Late thirties, dark shirt, sleeves pushed up, forearms on the desk, front-on to camera, chin level — not looking up, not hunched. |
| 50–64 | **The paper.** Four leaning towers, the tallest topping out at 18% from the top of frame — the stack skyline must clear her head by at least half a head. Not neat reams: curled, uneven, loose sheets fanning. Floor stacks at 60–68% along the bottom edge. |
| 69 | **The open case.** Hard metal toolcase, lid up at 60°, sat on the desk's near-right corner, centre at 62% down. Interior lit green from inside. Interior fitted out like a machinist's: dividers, calibrated tools, a logbook. |
| 76 / 84 / 90 / 95 | **The four specialists**, standing, in an arc receding away from camera at 3.2m / 4.5m / 6.0m / 8.0m. Heads at 30% / 34% / 37% / 39% down. Mixed ages, mixed builds. Each holds a closed metal case at their side. Each case shows a thin green lid-seam line roughly 5% of frame width long. The furthest is clipped by the right edge — he is in frame, not entering it. |

**Silhouettes, because that's what survives the downscale.** A: canvas machinist's apron, short sleeves. B: long unbuttoned lab coat. C: tailored overcoat, narrow. D: bulky field jacket, hood down. Four different outlines, legible at 12px tall. That is "each carrying their own method." Do not try to say it with faces.

**Light.**
- **Key:** one bare tungsten bulb in a steel shade, hung 2.2m above the desk, just out of top of frame. 2700K. Hard-edged pool ~2.4m across over the desk and paper, falling to nothing 2m out. The white paper bounces back up into her face — that is her only fill.
- **Accent:** the open case interior, 525nm green, bright enough to rim the near edges of the paper towers and put a green edge on her right jaw and right shoulder.
- **The three rear cases:** seam lines only, at about 1/8 the open case's intensity, each dimmer than the one in front.
- **Separation:** one cool wash at ~5% on the racking wall 14m back, heavily defocused, enough to edge the four figures' shoulders out of the black.

**Grade.** Warm pool, neutral skin, green accent, nothing in between. Black point at 2 IRE — do not lift the blacks. 85% of this frame is under 15% luminance and that is both the headline strategy and the file-size strategy.

**Say no to, in the prompt:** grain, film texture, volumetric haze, light shafts, lens flare, bokeh balls, reflective wet floor. Every one of those is high-frequency detail that the encoder pays for and the 300px viewer never sees.

**Paste-ready still prompt:**
> Elevated locked wide, 40mm, f/2.8, camera 2.6m high tilted 18° down. Dark concrete warehouse. Centre-right: a battered steel desk angled 25° to camera, a woman in her late thirties seated behind it, dark shirt, forearms on the desk, chin level. On the desk's near-right corner, an open metal toolcase, lid up, lit green from inside, its light rimming the paper and her right jaw. To the right, four people standing still in the dark at increasing distances — a machinist in a canvas apron, a figure in a long lab coat, a narrow tailored overcoat, a bulky field jacket — each holding a closed metal case with a thin green glowing lid seam. Background racking 14m back, very soft, cool, 5% brightness. Left third of frame empty dark floor. Deep crushed blacks, no haze, no grain, no flare. 16:9.

## 2. The four seconds, beat by beat

24fps, 96 frames. Two independent cycles, both of which land exactly on frame 96.

**Cycle A — the sheet conveyor. 32 frames, run three times.**
One sheet lifts off the top of the tallest tower, rises ~25cm, rotates roughly 90° about its long axis as it travels right and slightly toward camera, tips, and drops into the open case. On-screen travel: **18% of frame width**, from x=56% to x=69%. Three sheets are in flight at once, staggered 32 frames apart, so at frame 96 sheet 1 is where sheet 1 was at frame 0.

| Frame | Sheet 1 | Sheet 2 | Sheet 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | lifting | mid-arc | dropping in |
| 32 | mid-arc | dropping in | lifting |
| 64 | dropping in | lifting | mid-arc |
| 96 | = frame 0 | = frame 0 | = frame 0 |

Her right hand supports it: lift at the top of each 32-frame cycle, release at +6 frames, back down by +14, still until the next. Three small identical gestures. She is handing work off, not having it taken.

**Cycle B — the pulse down the line. Triggered on each drop, 32-frame period.**
A sheet lands in the near case → that case's interior blooms +30% over 4 frames and decays back over 14. Case B blooms 4 frames later, C 8 frames later, D 12 frames later. A signal running back through the roster. This is pure luminance on four small areas — no spatial motion, costs almost nothing, and it is the single most reliable thing an I2V model will give you.

**Cycle C — breath. One 96-frame cycle.**
Her shoulders lower ~3cm over frames 0–48 and return over 48–96. That is all. No head lift: a head lift in a loop has to come back down, and a head coming back down reads as defeat. A breath reads as relief and loops natively. Optional single blink at frame 62, 4 frames — drop it the moment the model smears her face.

**Loop point: frame 96 = frame 0.** Deliver frames 0–95 inclusive. Do **not** include both 0 and 96 or you get a held frame every four seconds, which is a visible cut by another name. If the generated plate drifts in exposure across the 96 frames (it will), fix it with a 4-frame cross-dissolve at the join — on a frame this still it is invisible, and it costs maybe 2KB.

**Nothing else moves.** No camera drift, no pendant sway, no dust, no breathing haze. If the model adds them, re-roll.

## 3. Why it reads at 300px and at 1600px

At 300px the frame is 300×169. Every storytelling element here is a luminance-contrast shape at least 3% of frame width, which is the only rule that matters.

**What you get at 300px — four reads, in this order:**
1. A bright pool in the middle of black with a small dark seated figure in it. *One person.*
2. A jagged pale skyline rising above her head. *Far more work than person.*
3. Four green marks stepping back into the dark, each smaller than the last. *Four specialists, and the line goes further than you can see.*
4. One white dash crossing right, every 1.3 seconds. *Work is moving off her desk.*

Her head is 12px. The case seams are 15px long. The travelling sheet is roughly 10×7px of near-white against near-black — the highest contrast thing in the frame, which is why it survives.

**What 1600px adds, and only adds:** the four trade silhouettes separating, the fitted-out interior of the near case, the green rim on her jaw, the curl on the loose sheets, her face. Nothing is *only* legible at 1600. Nothing is lost at 300 except texture.

**The left third** is flat dark floor with one soft vertical and zero motion. Headline sits on it clean, and it encodes at almost no cost.

## 4. What I'd cut if it comes back over 300KB

Budget is 300KB / 4s ≈ **600kbps**. Cut in this order, stopping when you're under:

1. **Any camera movement.** It's already cut. Global motion touches every macroblock in every frame and is by far the most expensive thing you can do. This is why there's no push-in.
2. **Dust, motes, atmosphere.** If the model added any, matte it out. Highest bytes-per-unit-of-read in the whole shot.
3. **Encode settings before content.** One GOP, no mid-clip keyframes:
   ```
   ffmpeg -i master.mov -an -c:v libvpx-vp9 -pass 1 -b:v 520k -g 96 -keyint_min 96 \
     -row-mt 1 -speed 4 -f null /dev/null
   ffmpeg -i master.mov -an -c:v libvpx-vp9 -pass 2 -b:v 520k -minrate 260k -maxrate 700k \
     -g 96 -keyint_min 96 -row-mt 1 -speed 0 -auto-alt-ref 1 -lag-in-frames 25 hero.webm
   ```
   h.264 fallback: `-c:v libx264 -crf 27 -preset veryslow -g 96 -keyint_min 96 -sc_threshold 0 -bf 3 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart -an`. Strip audio entirely — it saves bytes and removes any argument iOS Safari might have with autoplay.
4. **Crush the left third to true black.** Flat 0,0,0 regions are nearly free. Take the floor gradient out to 20% across.
5. **Three sheets → two.** Period becomes 48 frames. Costs you one beat of rhythm, saves a third of the moving pixels.
6. **Pulse only the two nearest cases.** C and D hold a steady seam.
7. **1280×720 plate, let the browser upscale to 1600.** On an image this dark and this soft nobody can tell, and it's a ~35% saving.
8. **Kill the blink.**
9. **Last resort: 3 seconds, 72 frames, sheet cycle 24 frames ×3.** 25% off. The loop gets noticeably tighter — I'd take options 1–8 first.

Poster image must be an exact export of frame 0, or you'll see a jump on first play: `<video autoplay loop muted playsinline preload="auto" poster="frame0.jpg">`.

## 5. Alternative treatment, three sentences

**Overload, distributed.** Locked on the same geography, but the paper is gone — her desk sits in a single tungsten pool blown so bright it's clipping, and the four specialists stand in black holding closed cases. Over four seconds the excess light bleeds out of her pool in four slow beams toward them, their case seams come up green as it arrives, and her pool settles back to a normal, readable exposure. Then it starts to overload again, which is both the loop and the honest truth about work — and because it's almost pure luminance with barely any spatial motion, it will encode at a fraction of the budget.

## Risks

- **The model will not reliably fly a sheet of paper.** This is the main one. I2V models melt thin rigid objects mid-arc. **Plan on splitting the shot:** generate the plate with breath + glow pulses only (both of which I2V does well), and composite the three sheets as 2D elements over it — flat white cards, 3D-rotated, motion-blurred, matched to the pool's colour temperature. Budget a compositor for this from the start rather than discovering it after twenty re-rolls.
- **Face drift.** Over 96 frames her features will warp. Mitigations in order: she's small, she's still, and if it still warps, matte her head region and hold it frozen — only the shoulders need to move.
- **Mobile crop.** If the page crops to 4:5 or 1:1 on a phone, the entire right side goes and with it three of the four specialists. This composition requires a 16:9 box at every breakpoint, or a different composition.
- **Headline colour.** This whole design assumes light text on a dark left third. Dark text inverts the entire lighting scheme and we start again.
- **Green in the brand.** If 525nm green is not an owned colour, four green lights is a strong commitment made by accident.
- **No permits, no weather, no talent** — the one genuine upside of generating it. The risk moves entirely into re-roll count, which is unpredictable; I will not give you a number of attempts, because nobody honestly can.

## Cost, said out loud

Nobody has mentioned a budget, so here are the line items rather than a figure I'd be making up: one still generation session plus a retoucher to clean the plate and fix the case interiors; generation credits for multiple 5s takes at the quality tier that holds a face; a compositor for the sheet elements, the loop join and the exposure match; an encode pass with the ladder above. The compositor is the real cost and the one most briefs forget. Price those against your own rates — if the answer is "we have no compositor," take the alternative treatment in §5 instead, because it's the version a model can deliver unassisted.
