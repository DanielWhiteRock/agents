## The narrative spine, in two sentences

At the top of the page the reader is the only person who can do any of the work in front of them; by the bottom they have seen four other people's methods running on their behalf, each one costing a number they can read.

What changes is not that they trust us, but that they no longer have to: the trace and the bill make the claim checkable, and the small numbers are printed rather than hidden.

# Which section to cut

**Cut 6, "New this week".**

It is the only section on the page whose content you cannot guarantee, and its default state is empty. With six authors, three of whom are placeholders, most weeks will produce nothing. An empty freshness rail is worse than no rail: it is the one element that reads as abandonment rather than as modesty, and it quietly contradicts everything the rest of the page is doing. The stat strip and the roles section make small numbers work for you because you chose to print them. An empty "this week" does not look chosen. It looks like nobody came in.

6b already does the job better. Novelty with an author attached is a trust signal; novelty on its own is just a date. If you want freshness, put one line at the top of 6b: "Most recently published: [worker] by [author]." That line is never empty, however slow the week.

**Runner-up, but do not cut it:** section 5. At three runs it is not a section, it is a feature flag, and it costs nothing while it stays dark. Keep it built. Just make sure the threshold is enforced in code and not by remembering.

# Where this house style costs you, on this page specifically

Said plainly, because some of it bites here.

**The display face.** Abril Fatface is the system's display type and it is right for exactly one thing on this page: the hero headline. Set all nine section headlines in it and a sceptical reader sees a brochure before they read a word. Use Abril Fatface for the hero only. Set every section headline in Inter 700. Set the stat strip figures in JetBrains Mono, because numbers you are asking to be believed should look measured rather than designed.

**The playful register.** The system's visual style is playful. Sections 2, 3 and 6b are the ones where you admit how small you are, and playful styling there reads as deflection. Leave those three plain: system surface, system text, no illustration, no colour fills. Let sections 1 and 4 carry the character.

**Colour that cannot carry text.** Against the system surface #FFFFFF, these fail the 4.5:1 body-text threshold and must be used as fills, borders or large display type only:

- success #16A34A — 3.30:1
- warning #D97706 — 3.19:1
- primary #3B82F6 — 3.69:1
- secondary #8B5CF6 — 4.25:1, close enough to be tempting, still a fail

Only danger #DC2626 at 4.83:1 and text #111827 at 17.74:1 carry body text on white. This matters most in section 7, where the live trace will want green for success and amber for cost warnings. Use them as status dots, left borders and backgrounds behind #111827 text. Never as the text itself.

**The hero's dark.** The palette has no dark surface token. Rather than inventing one, invert the two you have: surface becomes #111827, text becomes #FFFFFF. That is 17.74:1, and it keeps the green toolcase light as success #16A34A used purely as glow.

**Spacing.** The scale stops at 32. A nine-section landing page needs more vertical separation than 32px between sections or it reads as one continuous wall. Stack the token rather than inventing new values: 64 (32×2) between subsections, 96 (32×3) between major sections. No 48, no 80. Inventing one off-scale value is how the rhythm goes.

**Focus.** On the dark hero, a focus ring in primary #3B82F6 sits at 2.42:1 against #111827 and is hard to see. Use a #FFFFFF ring at 2px with a 2px offset on dark surfaces, and primary #3B82F6 on white. Both are system values. Check the hero buttons specifically, since they are the only interactive elements on an inverted surface.
